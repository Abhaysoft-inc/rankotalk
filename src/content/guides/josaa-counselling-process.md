JoSAA counselling is the single process through which JEE Main and JEE Advanced ranks are converted into seats. If you have a JEE Main rank, it decides your seat in NITs, IIITs and GFTIs. This guide explains each stage in the order you will meet it.

## What JoSAA is

JoSAA stands for Joint Seat Allocation Authority. It runs one common, round-based counselling for IITs, NITs, IIITs and Government Funded Technical Institutions (GFTIs). IIT seats are allotted on JEE Advanced rank. NIT, IIIT and GFTI seats are allotted on JEE Main rank. You register once and fill one choice list, and the system allots the best choice available for your rank in each round.

1. Register on the JoSAA portal.
2. Fill your choices of institute and branch in order of preference.
3. Lock your choices before the deadline.
4. Look at the mock allotments and adjust your list.
5. Get your round 1 allotment and respond to it.
6. Repeat for the later rounds.
7. After the last round, apply for CSAB special rounds if you still want a better seat.

## Step 1: Register

Registration opens on the official JoSAA website, usually in June after the JEE Advanced results. You log in with your JEE Main details, set a password and confirm your personal and exam details. Use your own email address and phone number, because every important message arrives there. Read the information bulletin published that year, because eligibility rules and dates can change.

## Step 2: Fill your choices

A choice is one branch at one institute, for example Computer Science at a particular NIT. You arrange your choices in order, with your most wanted seat at the top. There is no small fixed limit, so you can add as many choices as you genuinely want to consider. The order matters more than anything else, and the separate [choice filling strategy guide](/guides/josaa-choice-filling-strategy) shows how to build it.

## Step 3: Lock your choices

Before the deadline you must lock your choices. Once locked, they are used for allotment. If you forget to lock, the system may not use your list. Locking can be repeated in the days before the first round, so you can change your mind until the cut-off time given in the schedule.

## Step 4: Mock allotments

Before the first round, JoSAA usually releases mock allotments based on the choices you have filled so far. They are not real allotments. They show which seat you would get today, so you can see whether your list is working. Use them to move choices around or add safer ones.

## Step 5: Round allotment and your response

After each round, the system gives every candidate the highest choice on their list for which a seat is available at their rank. You then see one of these outcomes: a seat, or no seat. If you get a seat, you choose how to respond.

| Option | What it means | Who it suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Freeze | You accept this seat and want no further upgrade. | You are happy with this seat. |
| Float | You accept the seat but allow the system to upgrade you in later rounds if a higher choice becomes available. | You got a seat, but one of your higher choices may still open up. |
| Slide | You accept the seat and allow an upgrade to a better branch at the same institute only. | You like the institute, and want a better branch there. |

After choosing, you pay the seat acceptance fee online and upload the required documents. The institute then verifies them. The fee amount, the deadlines and any physical reporting requirement are published each year, so read the current notice.

Missing a deadline can cancel your seat and may take you out of the process, so keep track of every date.

## Step 6: Later rounds

JoSAA has run six rounds in recent years. In each round, candidates who chose Float or Slide may be upgraded, vacated seats are given to other candidates, and closing ranks usually relax. If you are happy with a seat you can Freeze it and stop worrying about later rounds. If you want to leave the process, there is a withdrawal option with a deadline, and refund rules are given in the bulletin.

## Step 7: After JoSAA, the CSAB special rounds

When JoSAA ends, seats are still left empty in some NITs, IIITs and GFTIs. The Central Seat Allocation Board (CSAB) fills these in special rounds. You have to register separately for CSAB, even if you took part in JoSAA. It is mostly useful if you did not get a seat, or if you want to try for a better one and are willing to leave your current seat.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Choosing seats in order of how likely they are, not in order of preference.
- Leaving the choice list short, and ending up with no seat.
- Missing the fee or document deadline.
- Using a certificate that is not in the required format.
- Selecting Freeze too early, when Float would have kept better options open.
