Your choice list is the only part of JoSAA counselling that you control fully. Your rank is fixed and the seats are fixed, but the order in which you list your choices decides which seat you end up with. This guide shows a simple method to build a list that works.

## How allotment actually works

In every round, the system looks at your list from the top. It gives you the first choice for which a seat is still available at your rank. It then stops. This has two important consequences.

1. **Order is everything.** If a seat you like less is higher on your list than a seat you like more, you can be allotted the one you like less, even though you would have qualified for both.
2. **You never lose a better choice by listing it high.** If you do not qualify for a seat at the top of your list, the system simply moves down. A dream choice at the top costs you nothing.

## Rule 1: Order by preference, not by probability

Write your list in the order you truly want the seats. Do not push a Safe seat to the top because it feels secure. If you would rather have a Reach seat, it goes above the Safe one. The system already handles the probability for you, by skipping seats that you cannot get.

## Rule 2: Make the list long

Fill more choices than you think you need. A long list means that you always have something to fall back on in every round. Include every institute and branch that you would honestly be willing to join. Do not add a seat that you would not join, because the system may allot it to you.

## Rule 3: Spread your list across Reach, Moderate and Safe

A useful shape for a list is:

1. **Top section: Reach.** Your dream branches and institutes, even if the odds are low.
2. **Middle section: Moderate.** Seats that are close to your rank, which will probably decide the final result.
3. **Bottom section: Safe.** Seats that you are very likely to get and would be glad to accept.

The [JEE Main college predictor](/jee-main-college-predictor) gives you these three groups for your rank, category, gender and home state. Use it to find candidates for each section, then arrange them in your own order of preference inside the list.

## Rule 4: Use your quota

If you are eligible for Home State quota at an NIT, your chances there are usually better than the same rank in the Other State group. Check the Home State options for your own state carefully, because they often include branches that you could not get elsewhere. Our [quota guide](/guides/home-state-vs-other-state-quota) explains how this works.

## Branch or college?

This is the question that students struggle with most. There is no universal answer, but these questions help.

- **How sure are you about the branch?** If you are certain you want computer science or a related field, choose the branch first and look for the best institute offering it. If you are not sure, a stronger institute usually gives you more room to explore.
- **What matters in the next four years?** Peers, faculty, labs, internships and location all vary from institute to institute.
- **Can you change later?** Some institutes allow branch change after the first year on the basis of grades, but the rules differ and are not a guarantee.
- **Do you want a core branch?** Mechanical, civil, electrical and chemical engineering have long histories and strong industries. They are often easier to get at a good institute than computer science.

A practical approach is to write both versions of your list, one branch-first and one college-first, look at where they differ, and talk through those differences with someone who knows you.

## Using the mock allotments and rounds

- Check the mock allotment and ask yourself whether you would happily join that seat. If you would not, change the list.
- After round 1, look at the closing ranks to see how the seats moved. Closing ranks tend to relax in later rounds as students upgrade or withdraw.
- If you got a seat you like but a better one may still open up, Float keeps the door open. If you got your dream seat, Freeze it.

## A worked example

Suppose a student is from Tamil Nadu, in the General category, and wants computer science most, with electronics as the next choice.

1. Reach: computer science at the most sought-after NITs, then computer science at a couple of top IIITs.
2. Moderate: computer science at mid-ranked NITs and GFTIs, then electronics at strong NITs, including the Home State seats at the NIT in Tamil Nadu.
3. Safe: electronics, electrical or mechanical at institutes where the student is comfortably inside the closing rank.

The student keeps the whole list in the order they want it, and the system gives them the highest seat available at each round.

## Mistakes to avoid

- A short list that leaves you with no seat in the early rounds.
- Putting seats you would not join anywhere on the list.
- Forgetting to lock the list before the deadline.
- Treating one year’s closing ranks as a guarantee.
